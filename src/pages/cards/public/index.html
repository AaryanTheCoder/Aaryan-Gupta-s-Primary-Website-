    A QUICK REFRESHER

    The table guide

    Empty your hand. Keep them guessing.

    1. Everyone gets a share of the shuffled deck. The admin chooses the first rank.
    2. On your turn, select 1–4 cards, choose an allowed rank, and play them face down. You may tell the truth or bluff.
    3. With “same rank,” keep claiming the last rank. With “one up/down,” claim the same rank, one lower, or one higher than the last claim. Ace is 1; King and Ace do not wrap.
    4. After each play, everyone else has 5 seconds to call Bluff. Only that latest play is revealed. A joker matches any claimed rank.
    5. A bluff? Its player takes the whole pile. Truthful cards? The caller takes it. The winner of the call chooses the next opening rank.
    6. You can pass. If everyone else passes, discard the pile; its last player opens again. An expired 30-second turn counts as a pass.
    7. Play your last cards and survive the call window to win. A truthful final play also wins if challenged.
    Two players? Before dealing, the admin can remove 10 random hidden cards once. This locks the game to two players. Jokers and rank rules are chosen before sending invitations.

    Two private cards. Five shared cards.

    1. Everyone begins with the chosen number of pretend chips. The dealer button moves clockwise each hand. Small and big blinds put in the opening bets.
    2. You receive two private cards. Betting happens before the flop (3 shared cards), the turn (1), and the river (1).
    3. Check if you owe nothing; call to match; bet/raise to increase; or fold to leave the hand. The amount box means your total bet for this betting round.
    4. A full raise must increase the current bet by at least the previous full raise (or the big blind). You can go all-in for less, but a short raise does not normally reopen raising for players who already acted. Several short raises can add up to reopen it.
    5. All-in players can only win the chips they matched. Extra bets form side pots. Unmatched chips return to their owner.
    6. At showdown, make your best five-card hand using any combination of your cards and the board—even all five shared cards. Ties split the pot; odd chips go clockwise from the dealer.
    7. After 30 seconds, you check automatically when possible; otherwise you fold. Win everyone’s chips to win the match.

    Hand rankings Strongest → weakest

    1. Royal flushA♥ K♥ Q♥ J♥ 10♥The highest straight flush.
    2. Straight flush9♠ 8♠ 7♠ 6♠ 5♠Five in order, same suit.
    3. Four of a kindK♠ K♥ K♦ K♣ 2♠Four matching ranks.
    4. Full houseQ♠ Q♥ Q♦ 6♣ 6♠Three of a kind and a pair.
    5. FlushA♦ J♦ 8♦ 5♦ 2♦Same suit, any ranks.
    6. Straight8♣ 7♦ 6♠ 5♥ 4♣Five in order, mixed suits.
    7. Three of a kind7♠ 7♥ 7♦ K♣ 2♠Three matching ranks.
    8. Two pairJ♠ J♥ 4♦ 4♣ A♠Two different pairs.
    9. One pair10♠ 10♥ A♦ 8♣ 3♠Two matching ranks.
    10. High cardA♣ J♦ 8♠ 5♥ 2♣No combination above.

    Aces can be high or low: A–2–3–4–5 is the lowest straight. Suits are equal; remaining high cards break ties. With two players, the dealer posts the small blind and acts first before the flop, then last afterward.

    The admin may end a game at any time. Ending Poker early returns bets from the unfinished hand. After a connection drop, your seat is held for 60 seconds while normal turns continue. Leaving Bluff ends that game; leaving Poker folds your hand. Calls are optional and may be blocked by restrictive networks. Rooms reset when the server restarts.
